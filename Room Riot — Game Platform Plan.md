# Room Riot

> **Everybody's got a phone. Nobody's in control.**

## 1. Concept

**Room Riot** is a locally hosted party-game platform inspired by games like Jackbox. One device—typically a PC, laptop, mini PC, or home server—hosts the game and displays the main experience on a TV or large screen.

Players join from their phones by visiting a local web address or scanning a QR code. No app installation or account is required.

### Core experience

1. Host launches **Room Riot**.
2. Host selects a game.
3. The TV displays a short room code and QR code.
4. Players scan the QR code or visit the Room Riot URL.
5. Players enter a display name and optionally choose an avatar.
6. Everyone appears in the lobby.
7. Host starts the game.
8. The TV becomes the shared game board while phones act as private controllers.
9. Players answer questions, vote, draw, bluff, sabotage, or make choices from their phones.
10. Scores are shown between rounds and a winner is crowned at the end.

**Room Riot**

The name works well because it describes both parts of the experience:

- **Room** — everyone is physically together.
- **Riot** — the games are chaotic, competitive, funny, and unpredictable.

# Game 1 — Hot Take

## Tagline

**Say what everyone else is afraid to say.**

## Players

3–12

## Type

Opinions / voting / prediction

## Concept

Players are presented with controversial, ridiculous, or harmlessly divisive questions.

Examples:

- What's the most overrated fast-food restaurant?
- Which fictional character would be the worst roommate?
- What household chore would you permanently eliminate?
- Which person in this room would survive longest in a zombie apocalypse?
- What's something everyone pretends to like?

Everyone secretly submits an answer.

The game then displays selected answers and players vote.

## Round Types

### Popular Opinion

Try to submit the answer most people will agree with.

### Hot Take

Try to submit an unpopular answer and defend it.

### Know the Room

Predict what the majority of the room will choose.

### Call Someone Out

Questions specifically involve the players.

Example:

> Who in this room is most likely to accidentally join a cult?

## Scoring

Points for:

- Matching the majority
- Successfully predicting the room
- Receiving votes
- Winning special prompts

# Game 2 — Suspect

## Tagline

**Everybody looks guilty.**

## Players

4–12

## Type

Social deduction / accusations

## Concept

Players answer questions about themselves privately.

The group then tries to determine which player submitted a particular answer.

Example:

The screen says:

> Someone in this room once pretended to be sick to avoid something.

Players secretly answer whether the statement applies to them.

One matching player is selected.

Everyone then votes for who they think it was.

## Special Rounds

### Alibi

The accused player can submit a short defense.

### Double Trouble

Two players match the prompt.

Players must identify both.

### False Accusation

Nobody matched the prompt.

Players can earn points by recognizing that the accusation is fake.

### Most Likely

Everyone votes on which player best fits a scenario.

## Scoring

Points for:

- Correct accusations
- Fooling the room
- Surviving accusations
- Correctly identifying fake prompts

---

# Game 3 — No Context

## Tagline

**It sounded better before we removed the context.**

## Players

3–12

## Type

Comedy / creative answers

## Concept

Players receive incomplete conversations, strange scenarios, screenshots, or statements and must fill in the missing context.

Example:

```text
"I can explain."

"What?"

___________
```

Players submit the funniest missing line.

The answers appear anonymously and everyone votes.

## Prompt Types

### Finish the Conversation

Complete a bizarre dialogue.

### Explain This

Players receive a strange image or situation and explain what happened.

### Bad Caption

Write captions for ridiculous images.

### Out of Context

Finish statements like:

> The police were surprisingly understanding about ________.

## Scoring

Points come primarily from player votes.

Bonus points can be awarded for unanimous votes.

---

# Game 4 — Groupthink

## Tagline

**Originality is overrated.**

## Players

3–20

## Type

Prediction / matching

## Concept

Players are given a category or question and attempt to submit the answer they believe **other players will also choose**.

Example:

> Name something people lose constantly.

Possible answers:

- Keys
- Phone
- Wallet
- Remote

Players receive points based on how many other people entered the same answer.

## Example

```text
KEYS — 5 players
PHONE — 3 players
REMOTE — 2 players
SOCK — 1 player
```

The five players who answered **Keys** receive the most points.

## Special Rounds

### Reverse Think

Submit an answer nobody else will choose.

### Top Three

Predict the three most popular responses.

### Know Your Friends

Questions specifically relate to the group.

---

# Game 5 — Drawn Out

## Tagline

**Art was a mistake.**

## Players

3–10

## Type

Drawing / guessing

## Concept

Players receive ridiculous drawing prompts on their phones.

Example:

> Draw Batman trying to return something at Walmart.

The drawings appear on the TV.

Other players attempt to determine the original prompt.

## Modes

### Classic

Guess the original drawing prompt.

### Telephone

One player receives a phrase.

Another player draws it.

Another describes the drawing.

Another draws that description.

The chain continues until the original phrase has been completely destroyed.

### Fake Artist

Everyone except one player receives the same drawing prompt.

Players take turns adding something to a shared drawing.

The player who doesn't know the prompt must try to blend in.

---

# Game 6 — Bluff

## Tagline

**Confidence is more important than being right.**

## Players

3–12

## Type

Trivia / deception

## Concept

Players receive unusual trivia questions where the real answer is difficult to know.

Example:

> In 1967, an Australian man was arrested after attempting to steal ________ using a forklift.

Players invent believable fake answers.

All submitted answers are mixed together with the real answer.

Everyone chooses what they believe is correct.

## Scoring

Players receive points for:

- Choosing the real answer
- Convincing another player to choose their fake answer

## Special Mechanic

Particularly convincing fake answers can earn a **Master Bullshitter** bonus.

---

# Game 7 — Friendly Fire

## Tagline

**Destroy your friendships responsibly.**

## Players

4–12

## Type

Roasting / voting / social

## Concept

Players answer questions about the people in the room.

Examples:

> Who would be the worst getaway driver?

> Who would survive the least amount of time on a reality show?

> Who is most likely to accidentally become internet famous?

> Who would sell everyone else out first during a zombie apocalypse?

Everyone votes privately.

The results appear dramatically on screen.

## Special Rounds

### Unanimous Decision

Huge bonus if everyone chooses the same person.

### Defend Yourself

The selected player gets 20 seconds to submit a defense.

The room votes whether the defense helped or made things worse.

### Return Fire

The targeted player gets to choose the next question.

---

# Game 8 — Last Word

## Tagline

**Think fast. Type faster.**

## Players

3–12

## Type

Fast-response / categories

## Concept

Players have only a few seconds to submit valid answers belonging to a category.

Example:

> Name a horror movie villain.

Players submit answers.

Duplicate answers are eliminated.

Example:

```text
Michael Myers — ❌ Duplicate
Michael Myers — ❌ Duplicate

Jason Voorhees — ✓
Ghostface — ✓
Freddy Krueger — ✓
The Nun — ✓
```

Unique answers score points.

## Round Variations

### Sudden Death

Players who submit duplicates are eliminated.

### Letter Lock

Answers must begin with a particular letter.

### Shrinking Clock

Each round gives players less time.

### Forbidden Answers

Several obvious answers are secretly banned.

# 6. Future Game Ideas

Once the core platform works, Room Riot could continuously add games.

## Caption This

Players caption memes, photos, or generated scenarios.

## Red Flag

Players build terrible dating profiles for each other.

## Hear Me Out

Players defend ridiculous opinions.

## Bad Advice

Players compete to give the worst possible advice to a problem.

## Plot Twist

Players are given the beginning of a story and submit ridiculous twists.

## Search History

Players invent fake search history entries for someone in the room.

## Under Pressure

Rapid-fire trivia where response time affects score.

## Majority Rules

Players try to predict how the room will vote.

## Almost Right

Players answer trivia questions where being closest matters more than knowing the exact answer.

Example:

> How many pounds does the average blue whale's heart weigh?

## One Upper

Players receive ridiculous scenarios and continuously attempt to outdo the previous response.

## Bad Influence

One player receives a situation and everyone else submits terrible suggestions for what they should do.

## Who Said That?

Quotes submitted earlier in the evening reappear and players try to identify who said them.

# 15. Game Engine

The most important architectural decision should be separating the **Room Riot engine** from individual games.

Each game should behave like a plugin.

Example structure:

```text
games/
│
├── hot-take/
│   ├── game.ts
│   ├── prompts.json
│   ├── config.json
│   └── assets/
│
├── bluff/
│   ├── game.ts
│   ├── questions.json
│   ├── config.json
│   └── assets/
│
├── groupthink/
│   └── ...
│
└── friendly-fire/
    └── ...
```

The Room Riot engine handles:

- Rooms
- Players
- Connections
- Timers
- Scores
- Game lifecycle
- Phone synchronization
- Display synchronization

Games only implement their specific rules.

# 24. Riot Mode

A special playlist mode could be one of Room Riot's standout features.

Instead of selecting individual games, the host chooses:

```text
START A RIOT
```

Room Riot automatically chooses short rounds from several games.

Example:

```text
ROUND 1
Hot Take

ROUND 2
Bluff

ROUND 3
Friendly Fire

ROUND 4
Groupthink

```

# 25. Chaos Cards

Another unique mechanic could be random modifiers called **Chaos Cards**.

They occasionally appear between rounds.

Examples:

### DOUBLE TROUBLE

All points next round are doubled.

### TARGET ACQUIRED

The current leader receives no points unless they win the round.

### REVENGE

Last place chooses the next category.

### ANONYMOUS

Player names disappear during voting.

### SPEED ROUND

The next timer is cut in half.

### BET IT ALL

Players secretly wager points before the next round.

### MUTINY

Everyone except first place receives a scoring bonus.

These could make Room Riot feel more chaotic and distinct from Jackbox.

# 30. What Makes Room Riot Different

The goal should not be simply making an open-source Jackbox clone.

Room Riot can differentiate itself through:

### Completely Local Hosting

No external Room Riot servers are necessary.

### No Accounts

Scan and play.

### Custom Content

Make games specifically about the people in the room.

### Extensible Games

Games operate as modules rather than being hardcoded into one application.

### Riot Mode

Mix multiple game types into one continuous competition.

### Chaos Cards

Random modifiers disrupt rounds and prevent games from becoming predictable.

### Persistent Game-Night Stats

Track the running jokes and disasters that happen throughout the night.

### Optional AI

Generate unique game nights around the players, occasion, or theme.
